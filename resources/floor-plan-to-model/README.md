# Floor-plan design contract

This is the **design-only foundation** of `FLOOR_PLAN_TO_MODEL`. Bubo validates a dimension-driven building specification and produces a complete architectural package of deterministic vector drawings. This stage does not construct meshes, launch Blender, export Blender/GLB/collision assets, certify traversal, or provide engine adapters. Empty-room architecture is the supported subject; furniture, interaction systems and gameplay remain outside this contract.

The supported design vocabulary is straight and angled wall segments, single- and multi-storey buildings, courtyards, openings, explicit slab voids, stairs, roofs, materials, clearances and finite output budgets. Curved architecture and automatic conversion of CAD files or raster plans are unsupported. Reference images may inform a human-authored design; they do not supply authoritative dimensions automatically.

## Ownership and source identity

The host selects the architectural style and passes its explicit identity with the request. Bubo does not discover a Hephaera installation, read its policy documents, select an engine or style, or invoke a host policy resolver. No new dependency, license acceptance or software installation is needed for these design operations.

The versioned structured plan is the dimensional source of truth. Stable identities describe semantic elements across drawings; an identity is not a substitute for a measured position or dimension. Preserve the exact input bytes and their hash. Treat a changed source, required drawing, style identity, validator/drawing implementation or runtime resource identity as a new design candidate with new dependent evidence. Do not overwrite an earlier package or rewrite its review history.

Property names are case-sensitive camelCase, and every declared property is required. Use explicit empty arrays for unused collections and explicit `null` only for nullable wall-room references and opening pivot/swing fields. Unknown properties, unresolved references and duplicate semantic identities are errors. Identities start with an ASCII letter and contain at most 96 ASCII letters, digits, underscores, dots or dashes; they are globally unique within each building. The plan includes `purpose`, `architecturalStyle` and the host-supplied `styleSha256`, material/detail-profile identities, an `entryRoomId`, the declared `player` clearance envelope and finite `budgets`. Every budget is positive, including the maximum allowances for stairs/openings when the actual collection is empty. Future export/triangle budget fields reserve limits for later stages; they do not enable an export operation in this foundation.

The native JSON reader limits the source to 8 MiB and nesting depth 64, rejects duplicate JSON properties, and accepts only canonical lowercase enum strings: `door`, `window`, `passage`, `start`, `end`, `left` and `right` in their corresponding fields. Numeric enum values and alternate spelling/case are invalid. Raw-byte, depth and duplicate-property checks complement the structural schema.

Every 2D point explicitly contains exactly `x` and `y`; every 3D point contains exactly `x`, `y` and `z`. The native point converters enforce canonical property case, finite numeric values and the complete axis set. A missing axis is invalid and never defaults to zero.

## Dimensional conventions

`schemaVersion` is `bubo-building-plan/v1`; `coordinateSystem` is `xy-metres-z-up`. Every length, coordinate and elevation is in metres, and angular tolerance is in degrees. Positive X points right/east, positive Y points up/north in plans, and positive Z points upward. One shared origin and elevation datum apply across every floor, roof, elevation and section. Do not reinterpret an upper floor in a different local origin or apply hidden unit conversion. Numeric values must be finite; zero, negative or unresolved dimensions are invalid wherever the contract requires a positive measurement. Physical dimensions and absolute coordinate magnitudes must also stay within `numerics.maxCoordinateMagnitude`; tighter field-specific limits still apply.

Each polygon has an implicit closing edge; do not repeat its first vertex. Outer rings wind counter-clockwise and hole rings wind clockwise in XY. Floor `elevation` describes the finished-floor level. The slab extends down by `slabThickness` from that level. Storey `height` is the clear vertical extent; the next finished floor must leave room for that extent and its own slab thickness. Room `boundary` polygons describe clear finished-face space, rather than usable space inferred by subtracting an unspecified wall thickness. Every room edge, including courtyard boundaries, must match an assigned finished wall face. Wall `start` and `end` describe its centerline, and `thickness` extends equally to both sides. Shared walls have one semantic wall identity. `leftRoomId` and `rightRoomId` are relative to the wall's start-to-end direction, with `null` for an exterior side. Openings belong to an explicit wall; `offset` measures along that direction and `sill`/`head` are relative to the owning finished floor.

Courtyards and stairwells require explicit void geometry. A room polygon does not silently remove a slab, and a courtyard label does not create an opening in another floor or roof. Footprint holes and named `slabOpenings` describe different declared cuts; preserve their ownership. Each roof is an upward-wound planar polygon with absolute Z coordinates. Roof geometry, level and complete coverage must be declared while preserving courtyard voids; use multiple roof planes around an open courtyard. Roof-plane projections may share edges but must not overlap. Stacked roofs with overlapping XY projections are unsupported in this foundation.

Each `stairs` record describes one straight flight between consecutive storeys. `start` and `end` are the lower and upper tread edges; horizontal run equals `(riserCount - 1) * treadDepth` and vertical rise equals `riserCount * riserHeight`. Width is perpendicular to that run. Each stair identifies its lower/upper storeys and rooms, actual landing polygons, headroom, material and required upper-storey slab-opening identity. Landings touch the tread edges outside the flight. The complete flight footprint must fit the declared upper-storey opening, a conservative design constraint. A drawn stair arrow is not evidence of a valid vertical connection.

Every stair requires `structureThickness`: the positive vertical waist-slab thickness beneath the pitch line through the riser bottoms. It must be at least `numerics.minimumFeatureSize`, at most one metre, and at most `numerics.maxCoordinateMagnitude`. At horizontal distance `s` from `start` along the run, the underside is `lowerStorey.elevation + (s / treadDepth) * riserHeight - structureThickness`. The underside starts below the lower finished floor by that thickness and ends below the upper finished floor by `riserHeight + structureThickness`. Tread index `i`, from zero through `riserCount - 2`, has walking height `lowerStorey.elevation + (i + 1) * riserHeight` over run interval `[i * treadDepth, (i + 1) * treadDepth]`.

Native validation compares the actual overlapping tread regions and waist-slab undersides of different flights for solid separation and declared headroom. Vertically stacked stair flights with overlapping XY footprints are supported when the actual vertical gap satisfies the lower flight's declared headroom; their shared projection alone is not a collision. Each flight's declared headroom must also fit the clear height of both connected storeys.

The flight must also fit both declared clear rooms, and its landings must stand clear of every slab void. Door/passage approaches retain the full opening width and at least one player diameter of clear space inside each adjacent room, without crossing a slab void. Player radius, height and landing depth must satisfy the declared minimum feature size. These checks establish geometric connectors; full navigation-mesh reachability, door-leaf sweep collision, live movement and jurisdictional building-code certification are outside this foundation.

JSON Schema checks the structural vocabulary and local scalar limits. Native validation remains authoritative for cross-field geometry: references and identity uniqueness, polygon validity, wall/room consistency, overlapping geometry, declared dimensions, opening intervals, floor alignment, roof coverage, slab cuts, stair connections, clearances and required-room reachability. Schema conformance alone cannot establish dimensional validity or architecture quality.

## Complete architectural package and review

Review one whole building candidate containing every dimensioned floor plan, roof plan, all exterior elevations and the required sections through vertical connections. Reviewers must be able to see room sizes, wall thicknesses, door/window locations, stair geometry, finished-floor levels, roof geometry and the relationship between storeys. Preserve drawing labels and dimensions as part of the package identity.

The drawing set contains `floor-01.svg` and subsequent floors in ascending elevation order, `roof-plan.svg`, `elevation-north.svg`, `elevation-south.svg`, `elevation-east.svg`, `elevation-west.svg`, `section-principal.svg`, and one `section-stair-01.svg` sequence for the stair axes in ordinal stair-ID order. The principal section is present even for a single-storey plan with no stairs. Dimensions use invariant formatting, while drawing data attributes preserve the exact declared values. Room schedules show labels, areas, bounds and edge lengths; wall/opening/stair schedules expose their declared dimensions. These drawing schedules support review of the complete geometry rather than creating new authoritative dimensions.

Human acceptance applies only to the exact complete package and its source/runtime identities. Acceptance of an individual floor, drawing, screenshot or metadata file cannot approve the building. Any plan revision invalidates whole-package acceptance and dependent model readiness, even when the revision changes only one floor. Preserve prior approvals as historical assertions and require a fresh complete-package review. A technical validation result or recorded assertion does not authenticate a human reviewer or establish visual quality.

Recording review preserves its exact evidence bytes in a hash-named immutable snapshot. Reusing a caller's live evidence path for a later review does not overwrite the earlier snapshot or relabel its decision. Changes to current live evidence invalidate its readiness assertion until fresh applicable evidence is recorded; retained historical bytes remain auditable.

Later construction and export stages must consume the approved exact candidate. Their editable Blender source, visual models, collision proxies, semantic metadata, re-import checks, independent review and separate human model acceptance are future work. Measured design clearances do not establish live engine collision, navigation or movement behavior.

## Resources

`building-plan.schema.json` documents the exact versioned JSON shape. The native .NET contract rejects unknown and incorrectly cased properties rather than silently accepting them. The example sources in `examples/` are serialized from the executable native fixtures and cover a single-storey design, connected multi-storey design, angled courtyard design and vertically stacked flights across three storeys. Their repeated `a` style hash is synthetic fixture data; replace it with the actual selected host style identity for production. These are engineering examples, not approved production buildings.
